## 3. Strongest geographic visibility gaps

The single largest, best-evidenced gap: **Mulberry Creek has no local-pack presence in any confirmed market other than Wills Point, on any of the four core commercial terms.** This dwarfs the previously-identified within-Wills-Point gap (absent on "plant nursery"/"landscaping company" there) — the new evidence shows the geographic gap is broader than the intent gap.

## 4. Does the competitive landscape change materially by city?

Yes, substantially. The nursery-line competitive set and the landscaping-line competitive set are each different in every market (consistent with the original Wills Point finding that these are two distinct competitive fields — now confirmed to also vary by geography within each field). Notably, Terrell and Forney's landscaping competitors overlap heavily with each other (RCD Outdoors, Triple-C, Olivos, Cabral all cross-appear), while Emory and Mineola's competitors are a mostly separate set (SNS Landscape, Texas Wunder Works, Hooten's, Halls Landscape and Design) — consistent with the two geographic clusters identified during call planning.

## 5. What can responsibly be concluded about Mulberry Creek's GBP/listing presence

**Nothing new can be responsibly concluded.** The business-listings call (Category I's previously-held call) was executed but returned a result set that excludes not only Mulberry Creek but also several independently-confirmed, active local competitors physically located within the same search radius (Triple-C Landscaping, Your Choice Landscaping, RCD Outdoors, Olivos, Cabral) — strong evidence the call's category filter or result set is incomplete, not that these businesses lack Google listings. Per the standing instruction not to draw a GBP conclusion without inspecting Mulberry Creek's own returned listing data, and none was returned, **the GBP-category hypothesis (Category I) remains unconfirmed** — this attempt did not resolve it. See `raw/multimarket_listings_2026-08-10.md` for the full reliability discussion.

## 6. Evidence for geographic/location-specific website pages

**Does not currently support building location pages for any of the 4 new markets.** A location page is normally justified by a combination of (a) confirmed demand and (b) some existing foothold or realistic path to visibility. Here:
- Demand: real but very low in absolute terms in all 5 directly-testable markets (Google Ads volume floor value, ~10-30/mo per term — see Section 3 below). Forney shows modestly more differentiated volume than the other four, the only market with non-empty CPC data.
- Visibility foothold: zero, in all 4 new markets, on every term tested.

Building a "Terrell" or "Forney" page today would be speculative — there's no current local-pack presence to reinforce and no page-level content basis (per the standing Category D gap) to build from. This is not a blanket "never" — Forney's somewhat stronger demand signal makes it the most plausible future candidate if visibility work in Wills Point succeeds first, but nothing in this evidence set justifies building it now.

**Elmo specifically:** no page can be justified or even discussed with real evidence — it isn't a resolvable search location at all, so there's no demand or visibility data of any kind to reason from.

## 7. Separate geographic recommendations for nursery / design / install?

**Not meaningfully separable from this evidence, because the outcome is uniform: absent everywhere outside Wills Point, for all three lines alike.** The one place a business-line distinction shows up is in the *competitor sets*, not in Mulberry Creek's own visibility: nursery-line demand (per Section 1's demand data) is technically present in all 5 markets at the same floor level as landscaping-line demand, and nursery competitors (Bruce Miller Farms, Moon Valley, Oak Tree, Grays) show a genuinely regional footprint pattern distinct from the more locally-clustered landscaping competitors (Olivos/Cabral in Forney specifically, Triple-C in Wills Point/Terrell specifically). That's a real, if modest, business-line difference in the *competitive structure* — not evidence that Mulberry Creek itself should pursue different geographic strategies per line at this stage.

## Local demand summary (Question B)

All 5 directly-testable markets returned real, non-empty Google Ads volume data for 4 of 5 keywords (plant nursery, garden center, landscape design, landscaping company) at the minimum reportable floor value — genuinely measurable, if very small, local search activity. "Landscape installation" returned no data in any market, consistent with its low national volume. Forney stands out as the only market with non-empty CPC data and the most variable monthly pattern, a directional (not conclusive) signal of somewhat more differentiated real query activity there than in the other four markets.

**This corrects the earlier finding that hyperlocal demand data was entirely unavailable.** That earlier conclusion was based on a different method (keyword-embedded geo-modifiers, e.g. "plant nursery Wills Point TX," at national location scope) which genuinely returned nothing. The location-parameterized method used here is different and did return real data. Per the standing instruction, the earlier empty result was never treated as proof of zero demand, and this new data confirms that caution was warranted — real demand exists, it's simply small at this hyperlocal scale, which is itself a legitimate, expected pattern for towns of this size and not a data-quality problem.
