Same $1,000 goal, three prices: one needs 67 customers, another needs 17. Here's the full math so you can run yours.

Most pricing advice starts with your costs, and that sets your floor. This post looks at the other side: how many people each price asks you to find.

Say your goal is $1,000 this month and you're choosing between $15, $35 and $60.

Step 1: customers
Divide the goal by the price and round up.
$15 → 67 customers
$35 → 29 customers
$60 → 17 customers

Step 2: visitors
Not everyone who visits your shop buys. If 2.5% of visitors buy (a fair starting guess; use your own rate once you have one):
67 customers takes 2,680 visitors
29 customers takes 1,160 visitors
17 customers takes 680 visitors

Step 3: views
If 7.5% of the people who see your posts tap through to your shop:
2,680 visitors takes about 35,700 views
1,160 visitors takes about 15,500 views
680 visitors takes about 9,100 views

The $60 price asks for about a quarter of the attention the $15 price does.

Why this helps
Each number is something you can plan around. 17 customers is a list you could write out by name. 35,700 views is a month of steady posting. Both can work, and they ask for very different weeks.

If your favorite price needs more views than you can get this month, you have three dials:
1. Raise the price and add value people can see.
2. Offer a second item that goes with the first, so each order is bigger.
3. Lift that 2.5% with clearer photos, a clearer offer and an easy checkout.
