The Problem

People Are Already Searching Pinterest For What You Sell

Pinterest isn't just a mood board. It's a search engine where people plan, save, and shop before they ever hit your website. Most small businesses either ignore it entirely or Pin randomly with no system behind it.

The hard part isn't making pretty graphics. It's knowing what people are actually searching for, whether that demand connects to what you sell, and where the click should go once someone finds you.

Social-First vs. Discovery-First

The Easiest Pinterest Mistake Is Importing Your Instagram Strategy

Social-first content chases followers and immediate engagement. Pinterest rewards discovery-first content: useful, evergreen ideas that keep earning traffic long after you post them. The workbook teaches the shift with the same product, framed two different ways.

Your SEO Co-Pilot

Your SEO Co-Pilot Is Built In

3 reusable "Ask AI" prompts throughout the workbook help you cluster research and diagnose results. They always work from evidence you provide, never inventing Pinterest search volume or popularity.

What AI is not used for here: guessing Pinterest search volume, inventing rankings, or attributing performance to "the algorithm" without evidence. Every prompt works from your real input and tells you exactly where to sanity-check the output.
✨ Ask AI

Section 8's Demand Universe prompt: a genuinely useful way to expand your own research, never to invent it.

Expand my seed keywords into the product, problem, outcome, style, use case, audience, occasion, season, planning, education, comparison, and gift angle categories. Label every suggestion as a hypothesis to verify inside Pinterest — do not invent Pinterest search volume.

If AI produces a number you didn't provide and it doesn't cite a real source, treat it as unverified.
