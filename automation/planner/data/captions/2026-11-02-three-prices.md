Your price comes from a pricing analysis. Here are the 12 things I'd work out before setting one. Save this for your next product 👇

Your costs for one sale
1. Materials and packaging for one item.
2. Platform and payment fees. Check your shop's fee page; most take a percentage of each order plus a few cents.
3. Any shipping you pay that the customer doesn't.
4. Your time: minutes per item × an hourly rate you'd accept. Count it even if you don't pay yourself yet.
5. A share of your monthly costs (software, website, samples): the month's total ÷ the items you expect to sell.

Your margin
6. Add up 1 to 5. That's your true cost per item.
7. Price minus true cost is your profit per sale. Profit ÷ price is your margin.
8. Leave room for a discount, a sale weekend or wholesale, so a lower price still sits above your true cost.

The market
9. Find 5 to 10 products like yours, for the same kind of buyer, and write down their prices.
10. List what makes yours different: materials, size, made to order, packaging, how fast it arrives.
11. Write down what it gives the buyer. A candle that arrives gift-wrapped saves a busy buyer a trip and a task.

Then your goal
12. Once the price is set, divide your income goal by it. That's the number of sales to plan your content around.

A worked example (a made-up candle):
Wax and jar $4.20, wick and label $0.80, packaging $1.50, fees $2.10, 15 minutes at $24 an hour $6.00, monthly costs share $1.40.
True cost: $16.00.
Similar candles sell for $28 to $38. This one is hand-poured and arrives gift-wrapped, so it sits in the upper half: $32.
Profit: $16 a candle, a 50% margin.
Goal: $1,000 at $32 is 32 sales this month.
