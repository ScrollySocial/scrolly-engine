Chat Manager setup guide

1. Before you start

Open the Chat Manager folder in Claude Code and say:

set up my Chat Manager

Claude asks a few questions (your brand, your colors, which platforms, where emails go) and writes your settings. When a step needs a key, Claude opens a small page on your computer where you paste it. Your keys stay in the .secrets folder on your computer and on your own server, so you never paste one into the chat.

You'll need: a card for DigitalOcean ($6 a month), your Instagram login, a Facebook login for Meta's developer site, and for YouTube the Google account that owns your channel. Plan on about an hour.

2. Your server (DigitalOcean)

  1. Sign up at digitalocean.com and add your card. New accounts sometimes ask for a quick identity check; that's normal.
  2. Go to API in the left menu (or cloud.digitalocean.com/account/api/tokens) → Generate New Token. Name it "Chat Manager", Full Access, 30 days is fine. Copy it.
  3. Paste it on the key page Claude opens. Claude checks it, then makes the server for you: the $6 plan (1 GB), in the city closest to you, with a firewall and everything Chat Manager needs.
The $4 server is too small. Chat Manager needs the $6 one (1 GB). Claude picks it for you.

3. Your web address (DuckDNS)

Meta and Google need a web address that starts with https://. You don't need a website: DuckDNS gives you a free one.

  1. Go to duckdns.org and sign in (Google, GitHub and others work).
  2. Type the name you want in sub domain (for example your brand) and click add domain. Your address is yourname.duckdns.org.
  3. Paste the token from the top of the page and your name on the key page. Claude points the address at your server, installs Chat Manager (5 to 10 minutes) and shows you your dashboard and your privacy page.

Have a website? You can use a subdomain like hooks.yoursite.com instead: add one "A record" at your domain company pointing to the server's address (Claude tells you the numbers).

On your dashboard's first visit, Claude shows your one-time setup code on your computer. Type it, then choose your password and save it in your password manager.

4. Instagram (Meta app)

This is the longest part. You're making a small app that only works with your own Instagram account, so there's no App Review and no business verification.

Your Instagram account

  1. It must be Professional (Creator or Business): Instagram → Settings → Account type and tools → Switch to professional account.
  2. Instagram → Settings → Messages and story replies → Message controls → Connected tools → Allow access to messages.

Make the app

  1. Go to developers.facebook.com and log in with Facebook.
"Account confirmation needed… unusual activity" on your first visit is normal for a new developer account. Click Confirm Account and follow the steps.
  1. My Apps → Create app. A popup says "There's a new way to create apps": click Create app.
  1. Name it "(your brand) Chat Manager" and add your email. Use case: Manage messaging & content on Instagram.
  1. Business portfolio: I don't want to connect a business portfolio yet. Then create the app (Meta may ask for your Facebook password again).

Add your Instagram account as a tester

Do this before "Add account". Give your Instagram account the Instagram Tester role, then accept the invite in Instagram. Pick Instagram Tester, which is a different role from Administrator, Developer and Tester.
  1. App roles → Roles → Add people → under "Additional roles" pick Instagram Tester → type your Instagram username → Add.
  1. Accept it in Instagram: instagram.com/accounts/manage_access → Tester invites → Accept.

Token, secret and webhook

  1. Use cases → Customize → API setup with Instagram login.
  1. Step 2 "Generate access tokens": Add account, sign in to Instagram and click Allow, then Generate token and copy it.
  1. At the top: Instagram app secret → Show, copy it. Paste the token and the secret on the key page.
  2. Step 3 "Configure webhooks": Callback URL https://yourname.duckdns.org/webhook/instagram. Verify token: Claude shows it on your computer with a Copy button. Click Verify and save.
Only comments subscribed (and messages if you turned DMs on). Meta pre-ticks several message fields: turn the others off. Then turn on your account's Webhook Subscription switch.
Skip step 4 "Set up Instagram business login" and step 5 "Complete app review". You don't need either for your own account.

App settings and Publish

  1. App settings → Basic. Fill in all of these:
    • Contact email
    • Privacy policy URL: https://yourname.duckdns.org/privacy
    • Terms of Service URL: https://yourname.duckdns.org/terms (easy to miss)
    • User data deletion: pick Data deletion instructions URL (the other choice is the callback) and paste https://yourname.duckdns.org/privacy#delete-your-data
    • App icon: a 1024 × 1024 PNG (your logo on a square; in Canva: Custom size 1024 × 1024, Download, PNG)
    • Category: Business and pages
    • App domains: yourname.duckdns.org
    Skip "Data Protection Officer". Click Save changes and wait for the green "Changes saved".
The Meta app icon starts no review. (Google's logo does: see the YouTube part.)
  1. Left menu → Publish.
Don't add anything on "Customize use case". The Publish page links to a list with "+ Add" buttons (Human Agent, Instagram Public Content Access, ads_management…). Extra permissions can send your app to App Review. Go back and click the blue Publish button.

Claude then turns on comment (and DM) notifications for your account and saves your account ID.

Two app IDs. The App ID on the dashboard is different from the Instagram app ID in API setup. Use the one a step asks for.

5. YouTube (Google Cloud)

Only if you want YouTube comments in your inbox and YouTube keywords. Nothing here needs billing.

Skip "Get started for free". That button is the $300 trial, and its step 2 asks for a card. Go straight to console.cloud.google.com and tick only the plain Terms of Service.
  1. Select a project → New project, name it "chat-manager", Create.
  1. APIs & Services → Library → search YouTube Data API v3 → Enable.
  1. Credentials → Create credentials → API key. In the wizard, "What data will you be accessing?" → Public data. Copy the key.
The "sensitive data… verification 4-6 weeks" popup in the wizard doesn't apply to your own channel. Pick Public data and carry on.
  1. Google Auth Platform → Get started: app name "(your brand) Chat Manager", your support email, Audience External, contact email, agree, Create.
  2. Branding: home page https://yourname.duckdns.org/, privacy policy https://yourname.duckdns.org/privacy, authorized domain yourname.duckdns.org. Save.
Never upload an App logo. A logo makes Google require verification, which takes 4 to 6 weeks.
  1. Audience → Publish app → Confirm, so it says In production.
Left in "Testing", the YouTube sign-in expires every 7 days and replies silently stop. Publish it. Never click "Submit for verification".
  1. Clients → Create client → Application type Desktop app, name "Chat Manager". Copy the Client ID and Client secret and paste them with the API key on the key page.
Untick "Use this client for an AI-powered agent" if it's there. Chat Manager doesn't need it.
  1. Claude opens a Google sign-in link. Sign in with the account that owns your channel.
"Google hasn't verified this app" is expected. It's your own app: click Advanced → Go to (your app name), then Allow.

6. TikTok (Apify)

TikTok doesn't let any tool reply for you, so TikTok comments arrive in your inbox with a suggested reply and a Copy & open TikTok button. They're read once a day through Apify.

  1. Make a free account at apify.com (it includes $5 of credit every month; Chat Manager caps each daily check at $0.10).
  2. Settings → API & Integrations → copy your Personal API token and paste it with your TikTok username on the key page.

8. Switch it on and test

  1. In your dashboard: Keywords → + New keyword. Add the freebie's name and link, pick where it works, read the preview, tick I approve these messages and switch it on. The checklist at the top shows what's left.
  2. Test mode is on at first: nothing is sent. From a second Instagram account (a friend's or a spare one), comment your keyword on one of your posts. Your inbox shows what would have been sent.
  3. Tell Claude to switch test mode off, then test once more: the DM arrives for real, and a signup shows up in your Email list.

9. Using it day to day

  • Inbox: every comment and DM worth answering, newest first. Open one, edit the suggested reply, press Send. TikTok: Copy & open TikTok.
  • Drafts on your Claude plan: say draft my comment replies and Claude writes a reply for each waiting comment. You still approve each one.
  • Keywords: one page per keyword with its stats.
  • Updates: download the new version from My Account → Downloads, unzip it over your folder and say update my Chat Manager. Your keywords, inbox, list and keys stay.
  • Check: check my Chat Manager tests every part and says what needs you.
  • Keep a copy of your .secrets folder somewhere safe: it's the only copy of your keys and your server key.

10. All the traps in one list

  • Google: never upload an app logo (4 to 6 weeks of verification).
  • Google: skip "Get started for free" (it's the $300 trial and asks for a card).
  • Google: Public data in the credentials wizard; the verification popup there doesn't apply.
  • Google: Publish app → In production, or replies stop every 7 days.
  • Google: untick "Use this client for an AI-powered agent". Never "Submit for verification".
  • Google: "hasn't verified this app" at sign-in is expected: Advanced → Go to.
  • Meta: "Account confirmation needed" on a first visit is normal.
  • Meta: Instagram Tester role first, and accept the invite in Instagram.
  • Meta: only comments (and messages) subscribed, and the Webhook Subscription switch on.
  • Meta: skip "business login" and "app review" steps.
  • Meta: all five Basic fields, including Terms of Service and "Data deletion instructions URL".
  • Meta: no "+ Add" features before Publish.
  • Meta: two different app IDs.
  • DigitalOcean: the $6 server, never the $4 one.
