# Reel Template Pack framework (read before every template, check before every showing)

Mercedes's rules for every Reel template in every pack (physical, digital, service, Halloween, fall, Thanksgiving,
Christmas). She has had to repeat these many times. **Every session working on a template reads this file first,
designs to it, and runs the checklist at the bottom before showing her anything.** If a storyboard fails one check,
fix it first; don't show it. When she adds a rule, add it here the same day.

## The rules (Mercedes, 2026-10-17, her words tidied)

1. **Visual hook instantly.** Frame 1 is already interesting and moving: no empty opener, no title card that sits there.
2. **Video-focused.** We are making Reels, not carousels. Footage (the buyer's clips) is on screen most of the time;
   graphics serve the video and move with it.
3. **Bold, creative fonts.** Lettering filled with patterns, magazine-style type, letters made of fun shapes or with
   fun backgrounds behind them (used sparingly). Never a plain default font as the hero.
4. **Emphasis on the transitions between clips.** Every clip change is a designed moment, not a hard cut by default.
5. **Interesting ways to split the screen.** Diagonals, shapes, windows, torn edges, objects that divide the frame.
6. **Layered backgrounds that change and help transition between videos.** The background is never the same all Reel;
   its layers move, peel, slide or open to carry one clip into the next.
7. **Oversized elements.** Big props, big words, things larger than life and partly off the frame.
8. **Outlined visuals.** Real images with what looks like a hand-drawn line around them.
9. **Real objects, played with, and many of them.** Not just an apple: an apple with an outline, a polka-dot overlay,
   a sticker or embossed label on it. Then lots of apples, each a little different.
10. **A blend of real photos, drawings and animation.** Real photographed objects (never objects drawn in code), a
    drawn playful layer (outlines, doodles, tape), and motion on everything.
11. **Text that looks handwritten** in places, as if someone is writing it as you watch (still readable over video).
12. **Real-looking paper:** post-it notes, note cards, letters, lined paper, labels.
13. **Every Reel has a purpose for someone who runs a business on Instagram** (an influencer or a business owner):
    a clear business job (sell the product, show quality, answer a buying question). Each pack has one audience:
    **the current pack is for physical product owners only.**

## Learned on templates 01 to 04 and the Halloween pack (keep)

- **Each template has its own world:** don't reuse another template's colours, backdrop or playful elements.
  #2 = kraft box, sage, washi tape. #3 = corkboard, push pins, label-maker tape, mint camera.
- **No 100% solid-colour backgrounds**, and never plain white. Use real textured backdrops, layered.
- **Layers interact with the video:** objects pass over it, the video breaks out of a frame, a prop half off-screen
  and see-through over the clip, the clip develops as a timer counts.
- **The story is logical frame to frame.** Act out the template's idea step by step (packing: label, folding, products
  in, extras, sealing, mailing). Don't show a result before its cause; don't repeat a frame.
- **Readability over video:** handwriting fonts (Ink Free) can't be read over footage on their own. Use bold type with
  an outline, or put the words on real paper (note, tape, label). **No dark gradient band behind words** (rejected on
  Halloween h2). Plain white text is "too boring".
- **No blurry hero props.** No clipart next to real photos. Nothing flying at the lens like it's being spat out.
- **Rejected looks:** code-drawn objects (boxes, magnifiers), emoji as main graphics, coloured outlines on objects in
  #2 (white die-cut edges only there), pink washi, Imbue condensed in cobalt with a glow.
- **Loved (#3, approved 2026-10-17):** big built-in words as Corben letters filled with polka dots in the hero prop's
  colour (the camera's mint), black edge, popping in one by one at slight angles.
- **Fonts and colours come from the scene's props** (e.g. the mint of the camera), and the accent text colour is the
  one buyers change to their brand.
- **CTA:** "Comment [KEYWORD]" (ManyChat style) is the default for most templates.
- **Sound matters:** recommend sounds per beat that are safe for business accounts; don't bundle files we can't license.
- **Process (she is short on time):** one concept, then the exact list of real images she makes in ChatGPT, then ONE
  storyboard round of stills built from those images with all feedback applied in one pass, then animate. Ask
  instead of guessing.

## Text placement and fonts (Mercedes, 2026-10-17)

- **No text under Instagram's buttons.** Text never sits where Instagram draws over a Reel: the top bar (top 220 px),
  the caption, username and audio strip (bottom 420 px) and the like/comment/share column (right 150 px, lower half).
  Images, stickers and other elements may go there. The build checks this: `make.py` prints an `IG SAFE ZONE:` line
  for any text that sits in a zone for 0.3 s or more (passing through on the way in or out is fine). Fix every one
  before showing her anything.
- **Two kinds of text, two kinds of font:**
  - **Text the buyer edits** (product name, shop name, offer, keyword, [brackets]) uses a font every buyer already has
    in CapCut (CapCut's library or a standard system font), so nobody has to install font files.
  - **Text the buyer won't edit** (the template's built-in words: "packing an order", "watch this", "sealed") can be
    EXTRA FUN, made as artwork: balloon letters, typewriter, letters filled with patterns, magazine cut-out letters,
    letters made of objects. Reference: Creative Besties on Instagram (all the crazy, fun fonts).

## Signature moves: reuse, but never more than 5 of 25 (Mercedes, 2026-10-17)

An approved move (the peanut drop-in flood, the box closing, the typing label, the torn-paper split) can be reused to
save time, but **one move appears in at most 5 of a pack's 25 templates**. Order in the pack doesn't matter, so back-to-back use is fine.

## From her other chats (collected 2026-10-17: #3, #4, Halloween, the Lisa breakdown, her own Reels)

**What the Reel is (her summary, #4 chat):** a written hook in a bold, fun font + a visual hook + layered backgrounds +
graphics on top (photos or videos with no background, emojis, drawings). The backgrounds help the clip changes: some
elements disappear, some take over, as part of the story. **The elements are the accent; the VIDEO of the person or
the product is the story.**

Hooks and words
- **A hook is a question or a stake** ("What would you choose?", "Watch this transformation"), not a theme or a label.
- **The first words are huge and visually dynamic** (animated, filling the frame). A small first line is not a hook
  ("once in a" in teeny type failed).
- **The main statement is the biggest thing on screen** with the most room around it; props never crowd it.
- **Fonts fit the Reel's mood:** holiday packs use a FUN, playful genre font like the inspo, not a plain or default
  one. Showing "a different font" that looks the same counts as not changing it. Show 3 to 4 real options rendered on
  a frame when the font is in question.
- **Don't invent arbitrary specifics** ("20 seconds") unless they serve the story.

Story and concept
- **A concept is a whole Reel with a selling job, not a theme or a look.** Ask "what is this Reel trying to do, and
  what's its CTA?" before designing. A seasonal look with no job is rejected (Halloween round 1).
- **Don't force a theme that isn't there** ("this isn't a school-themed Reel"). The world comes from the Reel's idea
  and the product, not from a decoration set.
- **No A/B or carousel-style ideas** (lid lifts and step cards that read as slides). It must be a video idea.
- **Enough video:** two moments of footage is not enough. Show what is actually happening (a cleaning timer shows the
  cleaning on it). The buyer's clips must not be cut off (the person reaches the floor) and products are never cropped
  awkwardly.
- **Visual jokes make literal sense at a glance.** Every noun can get a literal picture (Lisa's visual puns), but the
  picture explains the idea; it never winks at it.
- **One motif per Reel, with meaning,** returning every few seconds (Lisa: smileys = generic creators).

Motion and physics
- **Objects behave like the real thing:** a spider swings in diagonally on its thread and moves along the web lines,
  never just sits in the middle; a bat hangs from a branch, not from nothing; web lines line up with each other.
- **Transitions as shape takeovers:** e.g. the black background closes into the circle of the moon, rather than an
  unrelated object flying across. Zoom-outs that reveal the whole (Prezi style) work.
- **No objects spat at the lens** ("barfing projectile pumpkin"). Nothing blurry: props, bats, spiders all sharp.
- **One background video at a time;** a double video behind the animation was rejected.
- Things that must be read or recognised are big enough (the howling wolf was hard to see; the "bat signal" too tiny).

Consistency
- **Use the images she made, as made:** if she generated door #2, use door #2. Don't add elements that weren't in the
  approved layout (bats appeared in h6 uninvited). The setting stays the same across frames (the porch can't change).
- **Real objects must look good:** if a ChatGPT image is weak (whole candy sheets), use only the pieces she picked.
- Decorative lines never touch or cross each other or the text. Too many stickers reads juvenile; keep a few.
- Colours come from what is in the shot (Lisa: graphics never add a colour that isn't already there).

Holiday packs
- Keep faith themes (nativity, Silent Night) in the Christmas pack; never suggest cutting them.

Showing her work
- She judges visually: storyboard stills, then a preview MP4. Never a text description of a Reel.
- Use the side-by-side review page with notes per frame (`halloween/review.html` pattern); read her notes from the
  page; give full file paths when she needs to load images, and say exactly where to save them.
- Her manual choices on a review page beat these rules: flag, never undo.

## Checklist: run before showing any storyboard or preview

- [ ] Frame 1 hooks instantly and is already moving (rule 1).
- [ ] Footage is on screen most of the time; this is a Reel, not a carousel (2).
- [ ] The hero type is bold and creative (pattern, magazine, shapes), and readable (3).
- [ ] Every clip change is a designed transition (4).
- [ ] At least one interesting split screen (5).
- [ ] The background changes and its layers carry the transitions (6).
- [ ] Something is oversized (7).
- [ ] Real objects have hand-drawn outlines or play on them, and there are many of them, each a bit different (8, 9).
- [ ] Real photos + drawings + animation are all present; no object is drawn in code (10).
- [ ] Some words look handwritten; some sit on real-looking paper (11, 12).
- [ ] Its world is its own (not another template's look); the story is logical frame to frame.
- [ ] It has a clear business purpose for this pack's audience (physical product owners) (13).
- [ ] The first words are huge, animated and readable; the font is fun and fits the mood; no dark band behind text.
- [ ] Video is the story (more than two moments of it), uncropped; graphics are the accent.
- [ ] Every object moves like the real thing, is sharp, and sits on or hangs from something; nothing spat at the lens.
- [ ] Only the images she made and the elements in the approved layout; the setting stays the same across frames.
- [ ] Every joke or picture makes literal sense at a glance; no lines cross each other or the text.
- [ ] The build printed no `IG SAFE ZONE:` lines (no text under Instagram's top bar, caption or buttons).
- [ ] Editable text uses a font buyers already have; built-in words are extra-fun artwork lettering.
- [ ] Any reused move is under its 5-of-25 cap (see the moves table).
- [ ] Would someone say "wow, I've never seen that on my feed"? If not, it isn't ready.
