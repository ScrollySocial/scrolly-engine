## The insight
Under the FTC's 2024 rule, US businesses can ask for reviews and even offer a thank-you, but they can't make it depend on a positive review, post fake or AI-written reviews, hide family and staff connections, or buy fake followers; knowing violations can cost up to $53,088 each.

## Why it's worth a carousel
- Who it matters to: new sellers in the make-money-online space, where fake proof and bought testimonials are common.
- What it helps them do: collect real social proof the right way (asking for reviews is allowed).
- What's interesting: incentives are allowed, conditioned incentives aren't; family can review if they disclose.
- 5+ slides: what's allowed (ask, a no-strings thank-you, organizing reviews) → what's not (rewards for positive reviews only, fake/AI reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, threats to remove bad reviews, fake followers) → the penalty → how to ask for an honest review.
- Visual angle: allowed / not allowed cards; a review request message done right.
- Consistent with her method and values: yes, matches her "no fake proof" rule.
