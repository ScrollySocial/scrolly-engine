## Hook

So let's start with the rules.

## 1. The three rules every tool follows

**[Meta's Private Replies page, the Limitations part, each line highlighted as you say it]**

The DM someone gets after they comment is what Meta calls a private reply. This is Meta's own page for developers,
and there are three rules on it.

Rule one: you get one message per comment. One.

Rule two: that message has to go out within seven days of the comment. If the comment was on a Live, it has to go
out while you're still live.

Rule three: after that one message, you can only keep talking if they answer you. When they reply, you have 24 hours
from their reply to send more.

**[You, talking to camera]**

So here's what that means for you. That first message matters more than anything else in your setup.

If your first message just drops a link, that's the whole conversation. If your first message asks them to reply,
the second they answer, you've got 24 hours to send the link, a reminder, or ask them a question.

**[Your own DM thread: the keyword comment, then "Reply READY and I'll send it", then their READY, then the link]**

This is mine. Someone comments the word, my first message asks them to reply READY, and when they do, I can send
them the link. Every tool I'm about to show you works inside these same three rules.

## 2. Way one: Meta's free tool

**[Meta Business Suite on your laptop: Inbox, Automations, Create automation, Comment to message]**

The first way is free, and a lot of people don't know it exists. It's inside Meta Business Suite, on a computer. It
doesn't work in the phone app.

You go to Inbox, then Automations, then Create automation, and pick Comment to message. Meta says it sends "a message
reply to comments on your posts that contain specific keywords or phrases."

**[Your test: typing the keyword and the message, saving it]**

I set one up on my own account to see what it actually does.

So who is this for? If you've got one freebie, you only need to send a link, and you're not getting many comments

## 3. Way two: ManyChat

**[manychat.com/pricing, Monthly tab]**

The second way is ManyChat, the one most people have heard of. ManyChat is an official Meta partner, and it connects
to Instagram through the same official system that anyone can use. The three rules apply to ManyChat exactly the same
way.

Here's what it costs right now, paying monthly.

**[Zoom in on each plan as you say it]**

The free plan is zero dollars for 25 people a month. Essential is 17 dollars a month for 250 people. Pro is 39
dollars a month for 2,500 people. If you pay for a year up front, Essential works out to 14 a month and Pro to 29.

**[Zoom in on the "What's an Active Contact" answer]**

Those people are what ManyChat calls active contacts: each person you message with in a month counts once, however
many messages you send them. Go over your plan's number and you pay extra for each person above it.

**[You, talking to camera]**

So here's the thing to watch on the free plan. Say one Reel does well and 40 people comment your word. That's already
over 25.

What are you paying for with ManyChat? The drag-and-drop builder, the hosting, their support, and never having to
touch a setup screen. It also works on TikTok DMs and WhatsApp, which the other two ways don't.

So ManyChat is for you if you want it working today, you're happy paying every month, or you need more than
Instagram.

## 4. Way three: your own

**[Your Chat Manager dashboard, the Flows tab for one keyword]**

The third way is to run your own. This is mine. I made a whole video about building it with Claude, what it costs,
and the setup traps, so I'll link that here instead of repeating it.

**[Card to video 1]**

What I want to show you is how it uses those three rules.

**[Walk the flow cards from top to bottom as you say each step]**

Someone comments the word. My one allowed message asks them to reply READY. When they reply, that opens the 24-hour
window, so it sends them a link to a short signup page. If they haven't signed up an hour later, it sends one
reminder, still inside the 24 hours. And when they sign up, their email goes into my email list, in a group just for
that freebie.

**[You, talking to camera]**

It runs on a server that costs me six dollars a month, and there's no limit on how many people it talks to.

What I give up is time. With my kit, setup takes less than an hour, but there are real steps: a Meta app, a privacy policy page, and publishing the app.
And when something changes, I fix it myself.

So this one is for you if you want the emails and the follow-up, without a monthly bill that goes up every time more
people comment.

## 6. How to choose

**[You, talking to camera. The three questions appear one by one]**

If you're still not sure, ask yourself three questions.

One: how many people comment your word in a month? If it's more than 25, ManyChat's free plan won't cover it.

Two: do you want their email, or do you only want to send them a link? If it's only the link, Meta's free tool might
be all you need.

Three: would you rather pay every month, or spend under an hour on setup?

If you've got one freebie and a few comments a week, start with Meta's free tool. When it stops being enough, you'll
know exactly what you need next.
