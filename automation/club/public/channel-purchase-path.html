The five stages, in order

Content → Profile/link → Specific offer → Checkout or booking → Confirmation/delivery. A weak point at any stage loses people at that exact step — the worked examples in How to Use It walk through both a digital-product and a service business start to finish.

Choosing the right commerce tool for your offer

The right destination depends on what you're actually selling, not a single universal recommendation.

1A single payment page, no full store needed
Who it fitsA digital product, a single service package, or a simple physical item where you don't need a full catalog.
What it doesA tool like Stripe Payment Links creates one shareable checkout page per offer in a few minutes, no website required, and can automatically deliver a digital file or send an email confirmation after payment.
Where to find itStripe dashboard → Payment Links (or an equivalent from whatever payment processor you already use)
A test purchase completes and you receive both a payment notification and, for a digital product, the automated delivery email.
2A booking page, for anything that needs a conversation first
Who it fitsServices, consultations, appointments — anything where a price genuinely depends on a conversation.
What it doesA scheduling tool shows your real availability and confirms a time without back-and-forth messages, then sends both of you a confirmation with what to expect next.
Where to find itAny current booking/scheduling tool you already use, linked directly from your Featured section or bio
Booking a real test slot sends a confirmation to both the booker and you, with the correct time zone.
3A small product catalog
Who it fitsSeveral physical products where a single payment link isn't enough.
What it doesA hosted storefront (Shopify and similar) handles inventory, shipping, and a real cart — worth the setup overhead once you have more than a couple of items.
Where to find itWhatever storefront platform you already use or are setting up
A test order completes and shows correctly in your orders dashboard.

Is your product or service information actually clear?

A digital product, start to finish

Example: the resume-writing course from the Instagram & Facebook module.

Worked journey · digital product
1. Content
A Reel demonstrating the formatting fix (see Instagram & Facebook, section B) ends on "link in bio."
2. Profile / link
The bio link points directly at the course's sales page — not a homepage, not a "shop all products" page.
3. Specific offer
The sales page states the price, what's included, and who it's for in the first screen, with no scrolling required to find any of the three.
4. Checkout
A Stripe Payment Link (or equivalent) processes payment in 2 fields: email and card. No account creation required to buy.
5. Confirmation / delivery
An automatic email arrives within seconds containing the actual course access link, not just a receipt. This step is where digital products most often lose trust if it's delayed or missing.

A service, start to finish

Example: the fractional CFO from the LinkedIn module.

Worked journey · service
1. Content
A LinkedIn post on contribution margin ends with "reply 'margin' and I'll walk you through it" (see LinkedIn, section B).
2. Profile / link
The Featured section links a short "book a 20-minute margin review" page, not a general "contact me" form.
3. Specific offer
The booking page states exactly what happens in the 20 minutes and that it's free, so there's no ambiguity about being pitched into a sale immediately.
4. Booking
A scheduling tool shows real open slots and confirms a time without an email back-and-forth.
5. Confirmation / delivery
A confirmation email states the time, time zone, and what to bring (e.g. "have your last month of order data handy"), so the actual call starts productive instead of spent on logistics.

The shape is the same for any offer: name the specific thing, remove every unnecessary step between interest and the actual transaction, and confirm immediately in a way that sets up what happens next. What changes is only which tool sits at stage 4.

Proof and objections along the way

  • Real reviews or testimonials visible near the purchase decision, not buried on a separate page
  • A plain answer to the objection you hear most often, before they have to ask
  • Clear delivery, shipping, or scheduling expectations stated up front: surprises here cause abandoned carts and cancelled bookings alike

Bundles and packages, only if they genuinely help

A bundle is useful when it removes a real decision burden ("just get me the starter set") or reflects how customers actually buy. It's not useful as a way to inflate average order value artificially. A bundle that doesn't make sense as a single decision reads as pushy, not helpful.

How you'll know this section is done
  • You can name, in order, all five stages a real customer goes through for your actual offer
  • You've personally completed your own checkout or booking flow on your phone in the last 30 days
  • Your confirmation or delivery step actually tells the buyer what happens next, not just "thank you for your order"

What to watch

Traffic arriving, no add-to-cart or bookingsCheck clarity of the offer and pricing before assuming the traffic itself is bad.
Cart or booking started, not completedCheck for unexpected costs, confusing steps, or a broken mobile flow.
Purchases happening, but few repeat or referredA delivery or experience problem, not a discovery problem — a different fix entirely.
