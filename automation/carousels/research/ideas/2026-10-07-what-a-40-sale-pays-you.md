## The insight
The price your buyer pays and the money you keep are different numbers, and the gap depends on where you sell: on a $40 sale, Stripe's standard fee leaves $38.54, while Etsy's three fees leave $35.75.

## Why it's worth a carousel
- Who it matters to: anyone pricing a first product (her audience), especially choosing between their own checkout and a marketplace.
- What it helps them do: price with fees in mind, and read "revenue" correctly (her Playbook: revenue is before costs).
- What's interesting: Etsy charges three separate fees; the transaction fee includes shipping; processing applies to the order total incl. tax.
- 5+ slides: price → Stripe fee math → Etsy listing fee → transaction fee → processing fee → what's left → what to do with it (build fees into price; track revenue vs profit).
- Visual angle: a receipt that subtracts each fee line by line (style E, or D with the card overlay).
- Consistent with her method: yes, Playbook ch. 6 defines revenue as money paid "before your costs". $40 is her real Club price, used as the worked number.

## Verified facts

| Etsy Payments processing (US bank account): 3% + $0.25, assessed on the gross order amount incl. shipping and tax | https://www.etsy.com/legal/etsy-payments/ | last updated Jul 31, 2026 | current | | Varies by the seller's bank country |

Computed (arithmetic on the fees above; $40 digital item, no shipping, before sales tax, no ads):
- Stripe: 40 × 2.9% = $1.16, + $0.30 = $1.46 in fees → $38.54 kept.
- Etsy: $0.20 + (40 × 6.5% = $2.60) + (40 × 3% = $1.20, + $0.25 = $1.45) = $4.25 in fees → $35.75 kept.

## Interpretation (not proven by the sources)
- Building fees into your price is a reasonable takeaway; the sources only state the fees.
- Etsy brings its own shoppers; the higher fee buys access to them. Don't frame Etsy as "worse".

## Freshness / limits
- Fees change; re-check both pages on build day.
- Not covered: Etsy Offsite Ads, any shop setup fee, Stripe Tax, refunds/disputes. Say "before optional ads and taxes".
