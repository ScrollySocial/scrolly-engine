## The insight
Not getting a 1099-K doesn't mean the income is tax-free: platforms only send that form above $20,000 and 200 transactions, but the IRS says to report all income, and self-employment tax applies once net earnings reach $400.

## Why it's worth a carousel
- Who it matters to: US beginners making their first online sales (her audience), who often assume "no form = no tax".
- What it helps them do: know when the rules start to apply, and keep records from the first sale.
- What's interesting: the 1099-K threshold went to $600 and then back to $20,000 / 200 (retroactively) in 2025, which confused a lot of people; the $400 line is much lower than most expect.
- 5+ slides: the common assumption → the 1099-K rule now → IRS: report all income anyway → the $400 self-employment line → 15.3% and what it covers → practical step (track sales and costs from sale one; ask a tax pro about your situation).
- Visual angle: two thresholds on one ruler ($400 vs $20,000); a 1099-K form that isn't needed for the tax to apply.
- Consistent with her method: yes (revenue vs profit; "net earnings" is after costs).
