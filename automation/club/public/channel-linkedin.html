Channel Module

LinkedIn

A practical route from a post to a real conversation, for online B2B sellers with the offer you already have. Best fit when your buyer is a business or a working professional, not a general consumer.

Overview & Results Set Up How to Use It Your $1K Numbers Track & Improve

Who this works best for

Strong fit: consulting, agencies, B2B software, professional services, courses aimed at professionals, and physical or digital products bought by a business rather than an individual. It's a weak fit for general consumer products — save it for a buyer evaluating you in a professional context.

Why we included it, and what "fast" actually means here

LinkedIn can genuinely produce fast conversation and offer feedback inside 30 days — a real comment or DM exchange with a qualified prospect can happen within days of your first real post. What it usually can't do inside 30 days is close a considered B2B sale end to end, since a real business buying decision commonly takes longer than that, independent of anything you do. Treat this track as the fastest way to start real, qualified conversations and learn how your offer lands with the people who'd actually buy it — not as a promise of closed revenue by day 30.

Insight

A document post is your strongest starting format.

LinkedIn renders an uploaded PDF or slide deck as a swipeable card post. Each swipe extends how long someone spends with your post, and LinkedIn has confirmed that dwell time is a real input to how far a post is distributed.

LinkedIn's own Engineering Blog published "Understanding dwell time to improve LinkedIn feed ranking" (October 2024), confirming dwell time as a real feed-ranking input. That post documents dwell time as a general ranking mechanism, not a specific claim that document posts outperform every other format — the inference that a multi-page document earns more dwell time than a single post is widely repeated but not independently confirmed against LinkedIn's own data here.

How discovery turns into a real conversation

Someone sees a post or a genuine comment you left on their own feed → checks your profile, which reads like a business resource, not a resume → comments, DMs, or visits your Featured link → you move a real conversation toward a call or proposal. The step most sellers skip is the third one: a profile written for future employers, not for a prospect evaluating whether to talk to you.

What you'll create and test in the next 30 days

  • A profile rewritten to read like a business resource, with your Featured section pointed at your actual offer
  • A small, real set of posts mixing text and document formats around one clear area of expertise
  • Genuine comments on posts where your actual buyers are already active
  • At least one real conversation moved toward a specific next step (a call, a proposal), not left open-ended

Existing network vs. starting from zero

With a thin profile and no posting history, comments on other people's posts will do more of the early work than your own posts — you're borrowing someone else's audience's attention. With an existing professional network or past clients, direct outreach and a real case-study post can move faster, since some trust is already established.

We don't yet have our own members' verified results to share here. What's documented above (document-post dwell time, comment-driven visibility) is a real, sourced pattern in attention, not a confirmed revenue outcome.

LinkedIn genuinely rewards a much lower posting frequency than Instagram or TikTok — a rushed daily post here reads worse than a considered one twice a week.

Adjusting the workload: if 3 touches a week isn't realistic, keep the comment day and drop to one post a week rather than dropping comments — genuine engagement on other people's posts is often the highest-leverage, lowest-cost activity on this platform for a new account. DM, after someone comments or replies with the keyword
Hi [name], thanks for the comment on the margin post, that's a really common blind spot. Curious, is that something you're actively looking at right now, or more of a "someday" thing? No pitch, just trying to understand where you're at.

Why this works: it names a specific, credible insight instead of a generic greeting, ends on a direct ask instead of a vague "let's connect," and the DM opens with real context and a genuine question instead of pitching immediately.

A comment-based CTA (asking for a keyword reply) is a deliberate choice on LinkedIn, since comments themselves extend a post's reach the way sends do on Instagram — not confirmed by LinkedIn's own data in this module, but a widely and consistently reported pattern.

D. A real case-study post, honestly

Case study format
Situation: one sentence on the client's real starting problem.
What we did: two to three sentences, specific enough to be checkable, not "we optimized their strategy."
Result: a real, dated outcome. If you don't have permission to share a number, describe the change qualitatively instead of inventing one.
Takeaway: the one principle a reader could apply themselves, even if they never hire you.

Never invent a client result or a number you can't stand behind. If you don't have a real case study yet, use this exact structure with your own numbers instead (a personal before/after), clearly labeled as your own experience, not a client's.

Situation: good conversations, no proposals or bookings

  1. Where to look: your DM history for the last several real conversations, next to how many turned into a call, proposal, or booking.
  2. What this can and can't tell you: it tells you people are willing to talk. It can't by itself tell you whether the gap is timing, price, trust, or an unclear next step.
  3. Plausible explanations: the conversation never named a concrete next step, or it's genuinely early in a longer B2B decision — see the Overview section above.
  4. One change to test: end your next several good conversations with one specific, low-friction next step (a 15-minute call link, not "let me know if you're interested").
  5. What to compare after: the share of real conversations that reach a booked call over your next batch.

Situation: one format gets comments, another gets nothing

  1. Where to look: compare comments and DMs per post across your last several text posts and document posts.
  2. One change to test: add the same specific comment-based CTA to your next few posts in the quieter format.
  3. What to compare after: comments per post for that format over your next several posts.
