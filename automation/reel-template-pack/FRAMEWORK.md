# Reel Template Pack framework (read before every template, check before every showing)

Mercedes's rules for every Reel template in every pack (physical, digital, service, Halloween, fall, Thanksgiving,
Christmas). She has had to repeat these many times. **Every session working on a template reads this file first,
designs to it, and runs the checklist at the bottom before showing her anything.** If a storyboard fails one check,
fix it first; don't show it. When she adds a rule, add it here the same day.

## The rules (Mercedes, 2026-10-17, her words tidied)

1. **Visual hook instantly.** Frame 1 is already interesting and moving: no empty opener, no title card that sits there.
2. **Video-focused.** We are making Reels, not carousels. Footage (the buyer's clips) is on screen most of the time;
   graphics serve the video and move with it.
3. **Bold, creative fonts.** Lettering filled with patterns, magazine-style type, letters made of fun shapes or with
   fun backgrounds behind them (used sparingly). Never a plain default font as the hero.
4. **Emphasis on the transitions between clips.** Every clip change is a designed moment, not a hard cut by default.
5. **Interesting ways to split the screen.** Diagonals, shapes, windows, torn edges, objects that divide the frame.
6. **Layered backgrounds that change and help transition between videos.** The background is never the same all Reel;
   its layers move, peel, slide or open to carry one clip into the next.
7. **Oversized elements.** Big props, big words, things larger than life and partly off the frame.
8. **Outlined visuals.** Real images with what looks like a hand-drawn line around them.
9. **Real objects, played with, and many of them.** Not just an apple: an apple with an outline, a polka-dot overlay,
   a sticker or embossed label on it. Then lots of apples, each a little different.
10. **A blend of real photos, drawings and animation.** Real photographed objects (never objects drawn in code), a
    drawn playful layer (outlines, doodles, tape), and motion on everything.
11. **Text that looks handwritten** in places, as if someone is writing it as you watch (still readable over video).
12. **Real-looking paper:** post-it notes, note cards, letters, lined paper, labels.
13. **Every Reel has a purpose for someone who runs a business on Instagram** (an influencer or a business owner):
    a clear business job (sell the product, show quality, answer a buying question). Each pack has one audience:
    **the current pack is for physical product owners only.**

## Learned on templates 01 to 04 and the Halloween pack (keep)

- **Each template has its own world:** don't reuse another template's colours, backdrop or playful elements.
  #2 = kraft box, sage, washi tape. #3 = corkboard, push pins, label-maker tape, mint camera.
- **No 100% solid-colour backgrounds**, and never plain white. Use real textured backdrops, layered.
- **Layers interact with the video:** objects pass over it, the video breaks out of a frame, a prop half off-screen
  and see-through over the clip, the clip develops as a timer counts.
- **The story is logical frame to frame.** Act out the template's idea step by step (packing: label, folding, products
  in, extras, sealing, mailing). Don't show a result before its cause; don't repeat a frame.
- **Readability over video:** handwriting fonts (Ink Free) can't be read over footage on their own. Use bold type with
  an outline, or put the words on real paper (note, tape, label). **No dark gradient band behind words** (rejected on
  Halloween h2). Plain white text is "too boring".
- **No blurry hero props.** No clipart next to real photos. Nothing flying at the lens like it's being spat out.
- **Rejected looks:** code-drawn objects (boxes, magnifiers), emoji as main graphics, coloured outlines on objects in
  #2 (white die-cut edges only there), pink washi, Imbue condensed in cobalt with a glow.
- **Loved (#3, approved 2026-10-17):** big built-in words as Corben letters filled with polka dots in the hero prop's
  colour (the camera's mint), black edge, popping in one by one at slight angles.
- **Fonts and colours come from the scene's props** (e.g. the mint of the camera), and the accent text colour is the
  one buyers change to their brand.
- **CTA:** "Comment [KEYWORD]" (ManyChat style) is the default for most templates.
- **Sound matters:** recommend sounds per beat that are safe for business accounts; don't bundle files we can't license.
- **Process (she is short on time):** one concept, then the exact list of real images she makes in ChatGPT, then ONE
  storyboard round of stills built from those images with all feedback applied in one pass, then animate. Ask
  instead of guessing.

## Text placement and fonts (Mercedes, 2026-10-17)

- **No text under Instagram's buttons.** Text never sits where Instagram draws over a Reel: the top bar (top 220 px),
  the caption, username and audio strip (bottom 420 px) and the like/comment/share column (right 150 px, lower half).
  Images, stickers and other elements may go there. The build checks this: `make.py` prints an `IG SAFE ZONE:` line
  for any text that sits in a zone for 0.3 s or more (passing through on the way in or out is fine). Fix every one
  before showing her anything.
- **Two kinds of text, two kinds of font:**
  - **Text the buyer edits** (product name, shop name, offer, keyword, [brackets]) uses a font every buyer already has
    in CapCut (CapCut's library or a standard system font), so nobody has to install font files.
  - **Text the buyer won't edit** (the template's built-in words: "packing an order", "watch this", "sealed") can be
    EXTRA FUN, made as artwork: balloon letters, typewriter, letters filled with patterns, magazine cut-out letters,
    letters made of objects. Reference: Creative Besties on Instagram (all the crazy, fun fonts).

## From the 6-Reel technique library (Mercedes, 2026-10-17)

Source: `Scrolly Organic Engine/products/content-engine/reel-reference-breakdown.md` (all 6 Reels are @li.gorbunova).

- **Drag and drop, and it looks chic.** The buyer drops in their own clips and the template makes them look good.
  Don't design templates that need special filming or extra footage.
- **What the buyer changes (Mercedes, 2026-10-17):** their clips, the text, and maybe some colours for their brand.
  Keep the brand colour in places that are easy to change in CapCut (text fill and outline); artwork in the brand
  colour needs a simple way to switch it (to be decided).
  Text that can't be edited (built-in artwork words like BEFORE) must work for ANY brand: generic words, and
  colours that sit well next to any brand colour.
- **Real prop reveals the buyer must perform on camera** (peeling a sticky note, flipping a board): she'd rather
  not. At most 1 of 25, and only if it's clearly worth it.
- **Transitions: the library's list is a starting point, not a limit.** Video shrinking to a card, shape takeovers
  (star shards), pattern wipes (checker), photo floods and pieces wiping off are ideas to borrow; every template can
  still invent its own. Only a huge menu (around 100) could ever become the set to pick from.
- **Photo inside the letters** is OK when the buyer can swap the photo in CapCut, or the photo fits the Reel's theme
  so it never needs changing. Outlined prices and red pill labels are fine in the text kit too.

## The image library is shared (Mercedes, 2026-10-17)

Every image in `assets/props/source/` (physical, fall, halloween, doodles, tech, ...) can be used in any pack or
template: a fall leaf in a physical template, a tech prop in Halloween, doodles anywhere. The folders only sort them.
Each template still has its own overall look (its own world), so reuse images freely but don't copy another
template's whole scene.

## The look: collage (Mercedes, 2026-10-17, from her fall mood boards)

The overall aesthetic is **collage**, like a Pinterest mood board, but with **less going on in each frame** than her
boards (they're the style, not the density):
- Photos as rectangular tiles butted edge to edge or slightly overlapping; the buyer's clip is one tile, and tiles
  grow, slide or swap to become the next clip.
- A few real cut-out objects that **cross the tile seams** (a bow, wax seal, butterfly, acorn, pinecone, candle,
  plaid heart, street sign), so the layers feel stacked.
- One or two paper pieces with words (a postcard, a note card, a torn quote strip, a striped card with big type).
- Textile and pattern tiles mixed in (gingham, plaid, stars, stripes, knit).
- Palette from the scene (fall boards: rust, burgundy, cream, warm brown).
- Rough density per frame: 3 to 5 tiles, 2 to 3 cut-outs, 1 paper piece. Readable over everything; the video stays
  the story.
- **Animate the collage, hyper creatively:** text pops onto an element (a note, a tile, a tag), then the camera
  zooms into that element and it becomes the next clip; collage pieces switch from a still photo to video (or back);
  tiles flip, slide, peel or swap. Invent freely: every move should feel like the collage is alive.
- **Collage lettering she picked (f2, 2026-10-17), reusable in other packs:** the big word in Anton caps filled
  with a real textile photo (the rust plaid) with a thick cream edge, plus a small line above it in the tall serif
  (CapCut font id 7580184437816675601) in burgundy with a cream edge. No paper needed behind it; set it on a calm tile
  (cream knit) so it stands out. Rejected: Alfa Slab on a postcard ("homemade"), the serif on torn paper.
  Code: `build/fonts_f2.py` `hero2()`.
- **Collage pieces that worked:** tiles of plaid, cream cable knit, wheat field, leaves, gingham (her checkerboard
  doodle in rust/cream), corduroy, wool; cut-outs as a SET (three different acorns) placed on a contrasting tile
  (acorns vanish on leaves); a real kraft tag used ONCE as the emphasis; she liked the zoom into the card that becomes
  the clip and the tile animations.
- **Never the tall serif on a card, tag or paper** (she HATES it, said twice: torn paper in round 1, note card in
  f2 v2). On paper use clean condensed caps (Anton). The serif only works as the small line over the plaid-filled word.
- **Any words that are business-specific must be editable** ("small batch" isn't true for every business): built-in
  artwork words only for generic words (THE FALL DROP, COMMENT); everything else in a CapCut font with a stroke.
- **Balance the beats (Mercedes, 2026-10-17, on the #11/#20 motion tests):** a Reel mixes three kinds of beat:
  **one large full-frame video with accents** (several small accents in different parts of the screen), **full-screen
  words with no user video**, and collage moments. Not a collage in every frame.
- **Each pack looks like its own pack:** the physical pack must not look seasonal (the #11 test with wheat, sunflower,
  latte and amber tones "looks too much like fall"). Keep fall images for the fall pack.
- **Modern, not girly or dated:** the #11 test read "a bit too girly and like 7 years old trend wise" (watercolour
  washes, dotted bubbly letters). She didn't like the shape/block outlines there either (dark lines round the tiles,
  ink lines round the cut-outs).

## Her idea bank (Mercedes, 2026-10-17): use these when they fit

**In one line: mixed-media video collages, hyper interesting and visually hooky.**

More from her (2026-10-17; images in the shared library, any pack can use them; she sees them as fall ideas first):
- **Butterflies and moths:** transitions and effects with their wings (wings open to reveal a clip, wings as two
  halves of a split, a flutter that wipes the screen).
- **Clock tower:** a separator (outline its point in a colour); rises in from the bottom to split the screen in two;
  or its clock face becomes a circle video that the camera zooms into.
- **Glasses frames:** a different clip inside each lens. Effects: colour inside the lenses, greyscale outside; blurry
  outside, sharp inside; or another effect.
- **Patchwork quilt:** the transition is one fabric at a time disappearing into the video behind it, until the quilt
  is gone.
- **Gas street lamp:** a divider between two clips; the lamp slides across with the incoming clip, carrying it from
  partly on screen to full screen.
- **Cathedral (a video):** cut out along the steeple line for a split frame: it starts taking most of the screen and
  sinks to the bottom, the steeple the last thing to leave off the bottom edge.
- **Skyscrapers / skyline:** cut the buildings out of the sky; bring them in together, or put the buyer's clip in the
  sky behind the skyline.

- **Wheel or pie of videos:** a round wheel (or pie) cut into a few slices (fewer than a Wheel of Fortune, about 3
  to 6), each slice playing its own clip. The whole wheel rotates to bring the next slice up. It can carry one
  section of a Reel or be the whole Reel's theme. Works for a **clock** too (each hour wedge a clip; the hands sweep
  from slice to slice): fits #11 A day with it, and the fall pie.
- **Balloons of videos:** balloons floating up above a person, each balloon its own clip.
- **Intermissions = points of emphasis:** a full-screen background with HUGE text and no footage between clips, used
  to land the key point (a pause beat, a list title, a big statement). 100% OK; footage still carries most of the Reel.
- **Layers of photography as a transition:** a very simple video layer we ship (clouds, water, sky) plus many real
  cut-outs (raindrops, leaves, stars, petals) drifting over it; the cut-outs thicken, then thin out and fade to reveal
  the buyer's next clip. Works with drag-and-drop because the layers sit on their own tracks above the buyer's slots.
  Needs: the simple video must be one we own (AI-made or filmed), and the engine needs opacity keyframes (CapCut has
  them; not added to `engine.py` yet).

## Pattern and doodle style (Mercedes, 2026-10-17, from Creative Market references)

The references aren't free to use; they show the **style** only. Make our own in ChatGPT in this style.
- **Polka dots look hand-painted:** imperfect, slightly wobbly round dots, varied sizes, scattered (not a perfect
  grid), black on cream, or big chunky dots with rough edges. Never perfect computer circles.
- **Patterns are modern and bold:** big flat florals on dark or saturated grounds; marker textures (stripes,
  zigzags, checks, crosses, dots) that look drawn with a thick marker.
- **Doodles look drawn by hand with a marker or pen:** scribbles, arrows, bursts, speech bubbles, stars, hearts,
  crowns, circles and boxes for framing, underlines (straight, wavy, dotted), squiggles. Thick, slightly uneven
  lines with dry-brush edges. Watercolour blobs are fine too.
- She loved the **peanut drop-in and flood** (#2).

## From her other chats (collected 2026-10-17: #3, #4, Halloween, the Lisa breakdown, her own Reels)

**What the Reel is (her summary, #4 chat):** a written hook in a bold, fun font + a visual hook + layered backgrounds +
graphics on top (photos or videos with no background, emojis, drawings). The backgrounds help the clip changes: some
elements disappear, some take over, as part of the story. **The elements are the accent; the VIDEO of the person or
the product is the story.**

Hooks and words
- **A hook is a question or a stake** ("What would you choose?", "Watch this transformation"), not a theme or a label.
- **The first words are huge and visually dynamic** (animated, filling the frame). A small first line is not a hook
  ("once in a" in teeny type failed).
- **The main statement is the biggest thing on screen** with the most room around it; props never crowd it.
- **Fonts fit the Reel's mood:** holiday packs use a FUN, playful genre font like the inspo, not a plain or default
  one. Showing "a different font" that looks the same counts as not changing it. Show 3 to 4 real options rendered on
  a frame when the font is in question.
- **Don't invent arbitrary specifics** ("20 seconds") unless they serve the story.

Story and concept
- **A concept is a whole Reel with a selling job, not a theme or a look.** Ask "what is this Reel trying to do, and
  what's its CTA?" before designing. A seasonal look with no job is rejected (Halloween round 1).
- **Don't force a theme that isn't there** ("this isn't a school-themed Reel"). The world comes from the Reel's idea
  and the product, not from a decoration set.
- **No A/B or carousel-style ideas** (lid lifts and step cards that read as slides). It must be a video idea.
- **Enough video:** two moments of footage is not enough. Show what is actually happening (a cleaning timer shows the
  cleaning on it). The buyer's clips must not be cut off (the person reaches the floor) and products are never cropped
  awkwardly.
- **Visual jokes make literal sense at a glance.** Every noun can get a literal picture (Lisa's visual puns), but the
  picture explains the idea; it never winks at it.
- **One motif per Reel, with meaning,** returning every few seconds (Lisa: smileys = generic creators).

Motion and physics
- **Objects behave like the real thing:** a spider swings in diagonally on its thread and moves along the web lines,
  never just sits in the middle; a bat hangs from a branch, not from nothing; web lines line up with each other.
- **Transitions as shape takeovers:** e.g. the black background closes into the circle of the moon, rather than an
  unrelated object flying across. Zoom-outs that reveal the whole (Prezi style) work.
- **No objects spat at the lens** ("barfing projectile pumpkin"). Nothing blurry: props, bats, spiders all sharp.
- **One background video at a time;** a double video behind the animation was rejected.
- Things that must be read or recognised are big enough (the howling wolf was hard to see; the "bat signal" too tiny).

Consistency
- **Use the images she made, as made:** if she generated door #2, use door #2. Don't add elements that weren't in the
  approved layout (bats appeared in h6 uninvited). The setting stays the same across frames (the porch can't change).
- **Real objects must look good:** if a ChatGPT image is weak (whole candy sheets), use only the pieces she picked.
- Decorative lines never touch or cross each other or the text. Too many stickers reads juvenile; keep a few.
- Colours come from what is in the shot (Lisa: graphics never add a colour that isn't already there).
- **The backgrounds of one Reel tell one story** (batch 2 notes, 2026-10-17): complementary colour and texture
  across all frames, not a mix (sky-blue watercolour + terracotta watercolour + polka dots was rejected on #11). One
  texture that changes tone with the story works (her wash toned morning cream > noon butter > amber > night navy).
- **Variety in repeated objects:** not three clocks, not three identical trophies. Use different real objects that
  belong to the story (#11: latte, sunflower, hourglass, candle; #20: a trophy, a gem, a gold seal).
- **No visual jokes that can read sexual** (the banana "for scale" was rejected on #07), and no idea that buyers
  would find hard to film consistently.
- Fonts she rejected on built-in words: Coiny (banana fill), Sigmar One, DynaPuff ("a tad girly"), her marker
  numbers as review numbers. Show 3 to 4 lettering options on a sheet when the font is in question.

Holiday packs
- Keep faith themes (nativity, Silent Night) in the Christmas pack; never suggest cutting them.

Showing her work
- She judges visually and **can't picture motion from stills or wireframes** (2026-10-17). Show rough motion-test
  MP4s (8 to 12 s, stand-in footage, every transition and the layers moving) instead of still storyboards, then the
  CapCut build preview. Never a text description of a Reel.
- Use the side-by-side review page with notes per frame (`halloween/review.html` pattern); read her notes from the
  page; give full file paths when she needs to load images, and say exactly where to save them.
- Her manual choices on a review page beat these rules: flag, never undo.

## From the Fall pack, round 1 (Mercedes, 2026-10-17)

- **Several clips per screen** is a core layout: 2 or 3 clips at once, stacked, side by side or diagonal, in frames.
- **Fast cuts are a hook:** a "day in my life" run of about 4 clips in 2 seconds under big bold words.
- **Look through, then zoom in:** the clip first seen through something (airplane window, tree knot hole), then the
  camera zooms into the full image.
- **A real prop that rises and falls between clips** (a fringed blanket) and can grow into the full-page background.
- **Overlays on an object stay inside its shape** (dots, stars, stripes on an apple never spill off it).
- **Many props is fine; a random pile is not.** Her round 1 Fall stills were rejected as busy (leaves, tags, stickers,
  cards scattered everywhere). What works: a set of the same object, each one played with differently (her apples
  in `fall/inspo/02`: one with tiny painted dots, one with tiny stars, one wearing a lined sticker label with words),
  or one object repeated as a frame or a floor (leaves all round the clip, a screen full of stars, acorns or
  mushrooms all along the ground: `07`, `08`). The video stays the hero.
- **Real photo + hand-drawn, by default (rules 8 and 10, restated):** most props are real photos that also carry a
  hand-drawn touch: a marker outline that looks drawn by hand (wobbly, uneven width, slightly off the edge, not a
  smooth even ring like the round 1 Fall stills) or doodles drawn on or around them. Not 100% of props, but most. Check cut-outs: white objects on white backgrounds (cards,
  cups) need a careful cut, and holes (tag loops) must be see-through.

- **Vary the window shapes across a pack:** not every template circles (pie, clock, apples, cups all round was
  "too similar"). Mix rectangles, tall strips, grids, diagonals, torn edges, arches.
- **More ideas from her (Fall wireframes):** a collage that comes together piece by piece and then animates; a
  background video where things in the scene get outlined by hand (her example: skyscrapers traced) as the clips
  come in.

## Checklist: run before showing any storyboard or preview

- [ ] Frame 1 hooks instantly and is already moving (rule 1).
- [ ] Footage is on screen most of the time; this is a Reel, not a carousel (2).
- [ ] The hero type is bold and creative (pattern, magazine, shapes), and readable (3).
- [ ] Every clip change is a designed transition (4).
- [ ] At least one interesting split screen (5).
- [ ] The background changes and its layers carry the transitions (6).
- [ ] Something is oversized (7).
- [ ] Real objects have hand-drawn outlines or play on them, and there are many of them, each a bit different (8, 9).
- [ ] Real photos + drawings + animation are all present; no object is drawn in code (10).
- [ ] Some words look handwritten; some sit on real-looking paper (11, 12).
- [ ] Its world is its own (not another template's look); the story is logical frame to frame.
- [ ] It has a clear business purpose for this pack's audience (physical product owners) (13).
- [ ] The first words are huge, animated and readable; the font is fun and fits the mood; no dark band behind text.
- [ ] Video is the story (more than two moments of it), uncropped; graphics are the accent.
- [ ] Every object moves like the real thing, is sharp, and sits on or hangs from something; nothing spat at the lens.
- [ ] Only the images she made and the elements in the approved layout; the setting stays the same across frames.
- [ ] Every joke or picture makes literal sense at a glance; no lines cross each other or the text.
- [ ] The build printed no `IG SAFE ZONE:` lines (no text under Instagram's top bar, caption or buttons).
- [ ] Editable text uses a font buyers already have; built-in words are extra-fun artwork lettering.
- [ ] Any reused move is under its 5-of-25 cap (see the moves table).
- [ ] The buyer only drops in their clips: no special filming, no on-camera prop reveal (unless it's the 1 of 25).
- [ ] Any photo inside letters can be swapped by the buyer, or fits the theme as is.
- [ ] Props are a set (same object, each played with differently) or a frame/floor, not a random pile; cut-outs checked at full size (no white edges or blobs).
- [ ] Her doodles (source/doodles) are visibly used: at least 3 doodle moments per template, big enough to see on a
      phone, each with a job (arrow at the product, frame around it, underline, burst on the reveal, scribble beside a word).
- [ ] Would someone say "wow, I've never seen that on my feed"? If not, it isn't ready.
