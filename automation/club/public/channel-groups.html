Groups & Communities

Facebook Groups, Reddit, and niche forums are places people already gather to ask real questions and get real recommendations — including, sometimes, for exactly what you sell. This is the slowest, most manual channel here, and the one most likely to punish obvious self-promotion.

The rule that actually matters

Communities exist for their members, not for your business. The businesses that get real, sustained value participate as genuine members first — answering questions, being useful, showing up consistently — and mention what they sell rarely, only when it's a real, honest answer to what was asked.

A community that figures out you're only there to sell will turn on you fast, and moderators frequently ban obvious self-promotion outright. This isn't a free ad channel — treat it like one and it stops working immediately.

Finding communities actually worth your time

Recognizing a real opportunity

Direct recommendation request"Does anyone know a good X for Y?" — the clearest, most appropriate moment to mention what you sell, if it's genuinely relevant.
Problem you can actually solveSomeone describes a problem your product/service addresses — answer the problem first.
General discussionNot every thread is a selling opportunity — most aren't.

What a genuinely useful answer looks like

Question: "Any recommendations for candles that won't trigger my partner's allergies?" A useful reply explains what actually matters (fragrance-free vs. lightly scented, wick material, wax type), THEN mentions your product as one real option among the explanation — not "Try mine! [link]" with nothing else.

Measuring what this actually produces

This is genuinely the hardest channel here to measure precisely — most platforms don't give referral analytics from a Group or forum post. Track what you realistically can:

  • Direct messages or profile visits following a specific post
  • A rise in a specific, trackable referral link if you use one (where community rules allow links at all)
  • Repeat recognition — being remembered as "the person who actually knows about X" compounds slowly but is real
There's no traffic calculator for this channel: referral traffic from a Group, subreddit, or forum thread isn't something any platform reports back to you, so a calculator here would be a guess dressed up as a number. Published, reliable case-study numbers for community-based marketing are also genuinely scarce across the industry — we're not going to invent one to fill this space.

Staying in good standing

  • Re-read a community's rules periodically — they change
  • If a moderator removes a post, don't repost it elsewhere in the same community
  • Being useful consistently, without selling, is itself the long-term strategy here

Your first action

This week: find and join 2-3 real, active communities where your actual customers already gather — and just observe. Read the rules, watch how genuine members participate, before you post anything.
